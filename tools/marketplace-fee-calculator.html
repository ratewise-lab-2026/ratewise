Why 'the price on your listing' is not your profit

Every marketplace charges more than the number in its headline. On Etsy, a typical order pays a transaction fee, a payment-processing fee, a listing fee, a regulatory fee and — if the sale arrived through an off-site ad — a 15% off-site ads fee. Only then do you pay for the product itself.

Sellers routinely price a $35 item as if they keep $35. This calculator exists to show the number that actually lands in your bank account, before you set the price.

The formula behind the result

The calculator turns price and fees into a single linear equation, so it can also run backwards (what price do I need?). In plain English:

  • Revenue = item price + shipping the buyer pays
  • Marketplace fees = % transaction + % payment processing + fixed processing + listing + % regulatory + % ads + % off-site ads
  • Profit = revenue − marketplace fees − your item cost − monthly platform fee
  • Margin = profit ÷ revenue
  • Break-even item price = (item cost + fixed fees) ÷ (1 − all % fees combined). It assumes you charge no shipping; if the buyer pays shipping, the real break-even is lower.

If that last denominator gets close to zero, no price can save you — the fee percentages alone eat the entire sale. The calculator will show that as n/a, which is a signal to change how you sell, not just raise the price.

The fees sellers forget

  • Off-site ads are the silent killer. On Etsy, sellers earning under $10,000/year can opt out; above that, it is mandatory. An off-site ad sale gives away 15% (12% for higher-volume sellers) of the whole order — including shipping.
  • Payment processing applies to shipping too. Shipping money is not yours. It gets charged the percentage fee before it ever reaches you.
  • Repeat listing fees. $0.20 is charged every 4 months per listing, plus $0.20 each time you renew or relist after a sale.
  • Regulatory operating fees. Etsy adds a country-specific percentage in some markets, and it has been raised more than once. Check your own seller dashboard for the current figure.
  • Discounts come out of your margin, not the fee. A 20% off sale removes 20% of revenue but the fee percentages still apply to the lower price — so your profit falls faster than the discount.

Fee schedules change. Always confirm the exact numbers in your marketplace's seller help centre before pricing; this tool lets you edit every rate for that reason.

FAQ

Does Etsy charge a fee on shipping?

Yes. The 6.5% transaction fee applies to the item price plus shipping and gift-wrap charges, and payment processing applies to the full payment amount. That is why this calculator applies percentage fees to item + shipping, not just the item.

How much does Etsy take per sale in 2026?

For a typical US sale: a $0.20 listing fee, 6.5% transaction fee, 3% + $0.25 payment processing, a small regulatory fee that varies by location, and 15% (or 12%) if the sale came through an off-site ad. On a $35 item with $5.99 shipping that is roughly $10–$16 in fees depending on ads.

What is the break-even price?

The price at which profit is exactly zero. Below it you lose money on every sale even though the order looks successful. The calculator solves for it directly, so you can see the floor before you set a price.

Why does the calculator say n/a for break-even?

Because your combined percentage fees add up to 100% or more of revenue. No price can produce a profit — the fix is structural: drop the ad rate, negotiate costs, or sell the item somewhere with lower fees.

Is this accurate for my country?

The percentages are defaults, not gospel. Etsy, eBay and TikTok Shop all vary fees by country and seller level. Open the 'Fee assumptions' panel and replace every rate with the one shown in your seller dashboard; the maths will then be exact for you.
